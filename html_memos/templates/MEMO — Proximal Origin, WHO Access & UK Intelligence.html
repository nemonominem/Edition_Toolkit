Executive Summary

This memorandum documents the factual, publicly recorded connections between the Wellcome Trust, its Director Jeremy Farrar, the British domestic intelligence service (MI5), and the WHO's access negotiations with China in February 2020. The central figure bridging the intelligence and scientific worlds is Baroness Eliza Manningham-Buller (former MI5 Director General and Wellcome Chair). A secondary layer of significance is the role of Sir Andrew Parker (MI5 DG, 2013–2020), who activated MI5's pandemic plan in early February 2020 — concurrent with Farrar's mobilisation of the Proximal Origin paper and with Sir Patrick Vallance's appearance on Farrar's "fateful decision" email chain.

The central finding of this memo is that the Proximal Origin paper and the Lancet letter were not purely scientific endeavours. They were instruments of diplomatic and narrative control, timed precisely to the two critical windows in which WHO was negotiating access to China — and deployed at the moment the full WHO–China Joint Mission departed for Beijing with its Terms of Reference still unresolved, unpublished, and unknown to the US government.

SECTION 05 The Terms of Reference: Unresolved, Unpublished & Unknown to the US
⚠ Key Pressure Point 2
Feb 16–19 — The ToR Were Not Finalised & Were Never Published

This is the most consequential structural fact of the entire episode, and it has been almost entirely overlooked in public reporting.

Finding 1
The ToR were not agreed before the mission departed.

The full 25-person WHO–China Joint Mission departed on Feb 16 without finalised, publicly agreed Terms of Reference. The mission arrived in China still operating under terms that China had not fully confirmed. This meant that what the mission could investigate, where it could go, and what data it could access remained subject to Chinese discretion throughout.

Finding 2
The ToR were never published.

Unlike standard WHO mission protocols, the Terms of Reference governing the Feb 16–24 mission were never made public. The WHO published the mission's final report on Feb 28, but the ToR themselves — the document defining the mission's mandate, scope, and access rights — were withheld. This means there is no public record of what China agreed to allow, what it refused, and what constraints were placed on the investigators.

Finding 3
The US government did not know what was in the ToR.

US officials were not informed of the contents of the Terms of Reference. When US Embassy Beijing and State Department officials later sought to understand what the mission had been authorised to investigate, they found they had no access to the governing document. This was confirmed in the context of the 2021 WHO-convened origins study, when the US and 13 allied governments issued a joint statement expressing concern that the mission had operated without "independent" access and that the terms under which it operated had not been transparent.

Finding 4
The Proximal Origin posting and removal request must be read against this backdrop.

Farrar posted the paper on Feb 16 — the day the mission departed with unresolved ToR — and then immediately sought to pull it back to organise a press conference. This is the behaviour of someone managing a diplomatic instrument, not publishing a scientific paper. The paper needed to be available (to signal to China and WHO that the natural-origin narrative was publicly established) but controlled (to prevent open scientific challenge at a moment when the mission's access rights were still hostage to Chinese goodwill).
